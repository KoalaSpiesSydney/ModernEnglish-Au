Lesson 1 · Grammar

The passive in every tense

The passive is always the same two pieces. Only the first piece changes.

The formula: be + past participle
The tense lives in be. The second word — the past participle — never changes.
done · written · broken · sung · spoken

We use the passive when the action matters more than the person who did it, when we don't know who did it, or when we don't want to say. If we do want to name them, we add by + the person: by you.

Active → passive

ActivePassive
Someone read those love letters.Those love letters were read.
A million singers have sung this song.This song has been sung a million times.
You will do it.It will be done by you.

The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence. The verb becomes be + past participle, in the same tense.

Two things students often mix up.
Our love has been grown — passive, someone grew it.
Our love has been growing — active continuous, it grew by itself.
Same with they were broken (passive) and they were breaking (active).

Questions and negatives

Change be, never the participle.

+−?
It is written.It isn't written.Is it written?
It was done.It wasn't done.Was it done?
It has been done.It hasn't been done.Has it been done?
It can be done.It can't be done.Can it be done?

Lesson 2 · Grammar

The past participle

The passive needs the third form of the verb. Irregular verbs fall into families — learn the family, not the list.

Three forms: base · past simple · past participle
write · wrote · written — "Those words were written."

The families

1 · No change at all

  • cut · cut · cut
  • shut · shut · shut
  • set · set · set
  • bet · bet · bet
  • hit · hit · hit
  • read · read · read (spelling same, sound changes)

2 · Ends in -t or -d

  • send · sent · sent
  • bend · bent · bent
  • build · built · built
  • leave · left · left
  • sleep · slept · slept
  • feel · felt · felt
  • meet · met · met
  • hold · held · held
  • tell · told · told
  • say · said · said
  • hear · heard · heard
  • make · made · made

3 · -ought / -aught

  • bring · brought · brought
  • think · thought · thought
  • teach · taught · taught
  • catch · caught · caught

4 · Participle ends -en or -n

  • write · wrote · written
  • speak · spoke · spoken
  • steal · stole · stolen
  • choose · chose · chosen
  • break · broke · broken
  • wake · woke · woken
  • bite · bit · bitten
  • ride · rode · ridden
  • give · gave · given
  • take · took · taken
  • shake · shook · shaken
  • see · saw · seen
  • do · did · done

5 · -ow / -y → -own

  • know · knew · known
  • grow · grew · grown
  • throw · threw · thrown
  • blow · blew · blown
  • show · showed · shown
  • fly · flew · flown

6 · i · a · u

  • sing · sang · sung
  • drink · drank · drunk
  • ring · rang · rung
  • begin · began · begun

7 · Back to the base

  • become · became · become
  • come · came · come
  • run · ran · run

8 · The ones on their own

  • be · was / were · been
  • win · won · won
  • chase · chased · chased (regular)
Careful — the traps.
sit looks like family 1, but it is sit · sat · sat. Compare set · set · set.
wake is woken, not "waken". ride is rode · ridden, not "rid".
show is regular in the past (showed) but irregular in the participle (shown).

The Passive

English makes the passive with one simple formula: be + past participle.

The tense lives in be — is written, was written, has been written, will be written. The past participle never changes.
