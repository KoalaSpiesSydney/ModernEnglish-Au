Use of English — Test 2: before you start

Cambridge C1 Advanced
Pre-teaching · 20–30 min

Everything on this sheet is tested somewhere in Test 2. Work through it first and the test becomes a check on what you know rather than a guessing game. Teacher notes run down the right-hand margin.

1Suggest, recommend, advise, warn

These four verbs all mean roughly “tell someone what to do”, and they all behave differently afterwards. Part 4 of this test turns on the difference between two of them.

Verb + -ing + someone + to + that…
suggest yes
suggest going		 no yes
suggest (that) we go
recommend yes
recommend going		 no yes
recommend (that) we go
advise yes
advise going		 yes
advise us to go		 yes
advise (that) we go
warn only with against
warn against going		 yes
warn us (not) to go		 yes
warn (that) it is closed
The one error that costs marks.
  • She recommended me to try the restaurant.
  • She recommended trying the restaurant.
  • She recommended (that) I try the restaurant.
  • She advised me to try the restaurant.

Advise takes a person. Suggest and recommend do not — put the activity straight after them, or start a new clause.

Warn: two patterns worth memorising

  • He warned us about the traffic.  /  warned us of the danger.
  • He warned us not to leave the door open.

Teacher note

8–10 min. Board the four verbs before showing the table. Elicit one sentence for each and let the errors surface — recommend me to almost always appears.

CCQs
• After advise, do we say who was advised? (yes)
• After recommend, do we? (no)
• Which verb needs against before -ing? (warn)

Anticipated errors
*recommend me to try • *suggest me to go • *advise to go (no object) • *warn me about to leave

L1 note
Speakers of Romance languages transfer the object pattern to all four verbs, because their equivalents allow it. Name that out loud — it stops the error faster than correction does.

2Practice A — right or wrong?

Tick the correct sentences. Rewrite the wrong ones.

  1. The doctor recommended me to rest for a week.
  2. I suggest leaving before the traffic starts.
  3. She advised us to book early.
  4. They warned us the ice was thin.
  5. He suggested me a good hotel.
  6. The guide warned against swimming there.

Teacher note

5 min. Pairs, then open class. Ask why, not just right or wrong — the reason is the learning.

Items 1 and 5 are the two wrong ones. Item 4 is correct but looks wrong to many students because that is left out.

Teacher note

4 min. Drill the three past participles aloud. The test item depends on spotting that there is no object after the gap.

4Collocations to know before Part 1

  • risky behaviour (not hazardous / perilous)
  • a catalyst for change
  • open up opportunities
  • attract an audience
  • an integral part of something
  • encourage someone to do something
  • particularly + adjective
  • a chosen few
How Part 1 works. All four options usually mean almost the same thing. The question is never “what does this word mean?” but “which of these four does English actually put next to the word after the gap?” Read to the end of the sentence before you choose.

Teacher note

5 min. Give the noun, students supply the verb or adjective from the list. Then reverse it.

Stress the strategy line in the box — students who translate the four options instead of testing them against the neighbouring word lose marks here consistently.

5Small words — Part 2

Open cloze answers are almost always one of these: a preposition, an article, an auxiliary, a relative pronoun or a linker. The word immediately before the gap usually decides it.

  • an opportunity for something  |  an opportunity to do something
  • risks such as … (introducing examples)
  • protect someone from / against danger (both correct)
  • allow someone to do  |  let someone do
  • expose someone to something

Teacher note

4 min. Cover the bold words and elicit. The allow / let pair is worth an extra minute — it is the commonest slip at this level.

6Word building — Part 3

GivenYou needWhy
DISTINCTdistinctiveadjective after one of the most. Not distinguished, which means respected.
PROGRESSprogressivelyadverb before an adjective: progress → progressive → progressively.
DRAMAdramaticallyadverb. Watch the -ic before -ally.
PURSUEpursuitnoun in the fixed phrase in pursuit of. The e disappears.

Two questions to ask at every gap: which part of speech does the sentence need, and does it need to be plural or negative?

Teacher note

5 min. Build each word on the board one step at a time (drama → dramatic → dramatically) so the middle form is visible.

Negative prefixes are not tested in this test but come up in the next — worth mentioning.

7Practice B — one word in each gap

  1. The coach advised me train every day.
  2. She recommended the new restaurant.
  3. They warned us the storm.
  4. It offers opportunities creativity.
  5. The meeting be postponed until January.
  6. The client complained the delay.

Teacher note

4 min. Individually, then compare. Items 1, 5 and 6 rehearse Part 4 answers directly — do not give those away, just note who struggles.

Then: send them to the test. Part 1 first.
