Starting school in New South Wales

A guide for families enrolling a child in a NSW public school

A  Every child in New South Wales has the right to a place at their local public school. This is the school whose intake area, sometimes called the local enrolment area, includes the child’s home. If you are not sure which school is yours, you can search for your address using the School Finder tool on the Department of Education website. Please note that this guide applies to government schools only. Private and Catholic schools manage their own enrolments, so families interested in these schools will need to contact them directly.

B  Most children start school in Kindergarten, the first year of primary school. A child may begin Kindergarten at the start of the school year if they turn five on or before 31 July of that year. Families can choose to wait another year, but not indefinitely: by law, every child must be enrolled in school by their sixth birthday.

C  To apply, complete an application to enrol. Many schools now accept applications online, although paper forms are still available from the school office. Translated versions of the form can be downloaded to help families understand the questions, but the application itself must be completed in English. Staff at the school are happy to help if you have any difficulties, and schools can answer enrolment questions at any time of the year.

D  You will need to supply several documents with your application. These include your child’s birth certificate or other identity documents, and proof that you live at the address you have given, such as a council rates notice, a lease or a recent utility bill. You should also provide an immunisation history statement from the Australian Immunisation Register, together with copies of any court orders relating to the child and any medical or support plans.

E  Families may also apply to a school outside their intake area. However, whether a place is offered will depend on how much space the school has, and children who live locally are always given priority. A small number of high schools are selective, which means that entry is based on academic ability. Students hoping to join one of these schools in Year 7 must first sit the Selective High School Placement Test.

F  Before your child starts, the principal may invite you to a meeting to learn more about your child and any support they might need. Children with disability or additional learning needs can be supported at their local school, in a specialist support class or at a school for specific purposes. To discuss the options, call the Department’s information line on 131 536.

Working part-time at Harbourline Logistics

Information for staff

At Harbourline, we understand that full-time work does not suit everyone at every stage of life. Part-time employees work fewer than 38 hours a week, usually on a regular pattern of days and hours. If you move to part-time work, your manager will agree your hours and days with you before the change begins, and this agreement will be recorded in writing.

Part-time staff are entitled to the same conditions as full-time staff, but most entitlements are calculated on a pro-rata basis, according to the hours you work. This applies to paid annual leave and to personal/carer’s leave. For example, a full-time employee working 38 hours a week receives 76 hours of paid personal leave each year, so a colleague working 19 hours a week would receive 38 hours. There is one important exception: all employees, whether full-time or part-time, are entitled to 10 days of paid family and domestic violence leave each year, and this amount is not reduced for part-time staff.

Please be aware that part-time work is different from casual work. Casual employees have no guaranteed ongoing hours, and instead of paid leave they receive a casual loading, which is an extra amount added to their hourly rate of pay.

Under the National Employment Standards, employees who have completed at least 12 months of continuous service may request flexible working arrangements, such as reduced hours, different start and finish times or working from home. This right applies to employees who are pregnant, who are parents or carers of a child of school age or younger, who have caring responsibilities or a disability, who are aged 55 or over, or who are experiencing family and domestic violence. Requests must be made in writing and should explain both the change you are asking for and your reasons. We will respond in writing within 21 days.

We will always discuss a request with you before making a decision. A request can only be refused on reasonable business grounds, for example if the change would be too costly, if it would not be possible to reorganise the work among other staff, or if it would lead to a significant loss of productivity or a negative effect on customer service. Where we cannot agree to a request, we will try to suggest an alternative arrangement.

If a disagreement cannot be settled, we encourage you to speak to the Human Resources team first. If the matter is still not resolved, either you or the company may apply to the Fair Work Commission, which can help both sides reach an agreement. Further information is available from the Fair Work Infoline on 13 13 94.

Starting a business in Australia

A step-by-step guide for new business owners

Running your own business can be rewarding, but getting the basics right from the beginning will save you time and money later. The steps below will help you get started.

Start with some market research and a business plan. A business plan is a written document that sets out your goals, your strategy and your financial forecasts. It will change as your business grows, but it gives you a clear direction, and a lender such as a bank will usually want to see one before it approves a loan.

Next, decide on a business structure. The four structures most commonly used in Australia are a sole trader, a partnership, a company and a trust. Each has advantages and disadvantages, and your choice will affect your tax, your legal responsibilities and the registrations you need, so it is worth getting professional advice. Remember that you can change your structure later as your business develops.

Most businesses need an Australian Business Number (ABN), a unique number that identifies your business to the government and the public. You can apply for an ABN free of charge through the Australian Business Register, but only if you are carrying on an enterprise. If your details change, you must update the register within 28 days. An ABN does not replace your tax file number.

If you plan to trade under a name other than your own, you will need to register a business name with the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC). You can register your ABN and business name at the same time using the government’s Business Registration Service.

You should also check which taxes apply to you. For example, you must register for goods and services tax (GST) once your business has an annual turnover of $75,000 or more. You may also need to register for PAYG withholding if you employ staff.

Finally, make sure you have the right licences, permits and insurance. The Australian Business Licence and Information Service (ABLIS) is a free online tool that lists the licences and permits your business may need, based on your industry and location. Some types of insurance are compulsory, such as workers’ compensation if you have employees, while others are optional but can protect you, your customers and your assets.

Storing the sun: the second life of solar thermal power

Solar panels now cover millions of rooftops, but they are not the only way to turn sunshine into electricity. An older, less familiar technology is attracting new interest because of one special ability: it can keep working after dark.

A  When most people think of solar energy, they picture photovoltaic (PV) panels, which convert sunlight directly into electricity. Thanks to mass production, these panels have become cheap and are now found on homes, farms and factories across the world. Their weakness, however, is obvious. Output falls as clouds pass over and stops completely when the sun goes down, which is often just as households switch on lights, heaters and televisions. Batteries can fill part of this gap, but most battery systems in use today store only a few hours of energy.

B  Concentrated solar power (CSP), also known as solar thermal power, works in a different way. In a tower system, thousands of flat mirrors called heliostats follow the sun across the sky and reflect its light onto a receiver at the top of a central tower. There, the concentrated sunlight heats a molten salt, a mixture of sodium nitrate and potassium nitrate, to around 565°C. When electricity is required, the hot salt passes through a heat exchanger, where it boils water to produce steam. The steam spins a turbine connected to a generator, just as in a conventional coal or gas power station, and the electricity is then sent to the grid. The salt, now cooled to about 260°C, returns to a separate tank before being pumped back up the tower to be heated again.

C  The key advantage of this design is storage. The hot salt is kept in a large insulated tank, and according to the international research network SolarPACES, it loses only about one degree of heat per day. As a result, a plant can collect energy during the day and release it hours later, during the evening peak or even through the night. The salt itself is also remarkably durable and can be heated and cooled daily for at least 30 years. The Crescent Dunes plant in the US state of Nevada, for instance, was built with a capacity of 110 megawatts and a storage tank able to hold ten hours of energy.

D  The largest example of the technology is found on the edge of the Sahara Desert, near the Moroccan city of Ouarzazate. The Noor Ouarzazate complex was built in four stages and has a combined capacity of 580 megawatts. The first three stages all use solar thermal technology: Noor I and Noor II rely on long curved mirrors known as parabolic troughs, while Noor III uses a central tower. Noor II and Noor III can each store around seven hours of energy in molten salt, compared with three hours at Noor I. The fourth stage, Noor IV, uses photovoltaic panels. Construction began in 2013 and all stages were running by 2018. Around 1,000 people were employed during construction, and Noor II and Noor III together are estimated to prevent around 533,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions each year.

E  Despite these successes, solar thermal power has struggled to compete. As the price of photovoltaic panels fell sharply over the past fifteen years, investors increasingly chose the cheaper option, and several planned solar thermal projects around the world were cancelled. The technology has also suffered technical problems. Crescent Dunes, once seen as a flagship project, was shut down for a long period after a leak was found in its hot salt tank, which damaged confidence in the industry.

F  One way the industry is responding is by combining the two technologies rather than treating them as rivals. In the Hami region of Xinjiang, in north-western China, China Energy Engineering Corporation is building a 1,500-megawatt complex made up of 1,350 megawatts of photovoltaic panels and a 150-megawatt solar thermal plant with a 219-metre tower. The idea is simple: the low-cost panels supply electricity during the day, while the solar thermal unit stores heat in molten salt and releases it in the evening and overnight. The site covers 33 square kilometres, and its engineers have designed protective structures for the heliostats that are expected to reduce mirror breakage by 90 per cent. Construction of the solar thermal unit began in late 2024, with full operation planned for October 2026.

G  Australia, one of the sunniest countries on Earth, is now testing whether the technology can succeed there. Port Augusta in South Australia was once home to coal-fired power stations, and the town is now the planned site of VS1, a solar thermal plant developed by the company Vast. The project is designed to have a capacity of 30 megawatts and to store 288 megawatt-hours of energy. The Australian Renewable Energy Agency (ARENA) has committed $65 million towards its total cost of around $203 million, and the project is expected to create up to 450 jobs during construction and up to 70 ongoing jobs. Supporters hope that it will show that solar thermal power can supply clean electricity around the clock.
