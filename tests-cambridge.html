C1 Advanced · Timed · Instant Feedback

Mini Cambridge C1 Advanced tests covering Reading and Use of English, plus combined listening and essay-writing practice, with Listening tests on the way. Each one is a 20–40 minute snapshot of the real exam — use it as a quick warm-up, a mid-course check, or a short diagnostic before students commit to a full CAE course. The skills, themes and vocabulary also carry straight over to IELTS and PTE.

Plan your time across the paper

The full Reading and Use of English paper gives you 90 minutes for eight parts. Many students aim for about 30 minutes on the four Use of English parts, leaving an hour for the longer reading texts.

Key word transformations: follow the rules exactly

Use between three and six words, including the word given, and never change that word. Contractions count as two words, so check your total before you move on.

Read the whole sentence before you fill a gap

In the cloze and word formation parts, the words after the gap often decide the answer, such as a preposition, a plural or a negative meaning. Read past the gap before choosing.

Spelling counts

In open cloze, word formation and key word transformations, a misspelt answer gets no marks. Learn word families together, such as decide, decision, decisive, indecisive.

Never leave a blank

There are no marks taken off for wrong answers, so always write something, even if you are guessing.

How to use these mini tests

Each mini test takes 20–40 minutes, so it fits into a single lesson, a homework slot or a short self-study session.

For teachers

  • Part of a lesson: project a test on the board and work through it together, using the pre-lessons and explanations to teach the skill behind each question type. Students can scan the QR code to open their own copy on their device.
  • A team warm-up: divide the class into teams, keep score on the board and give feedback after each question.
  • A snap test: run one at the start or end of class to check progress. Students see their score on their phone, so they can show you or send you a screenshot.
  • Homework: set a test to complete at home and ask students to send a screenshot of their score.
  • A course check-in: use one before and after a unit to see what has improved.

For students

The mini tests are ideal for self-study. First work through a test untimed, reading the tips and explanations. Then press Start and do it again under exam timing. Doing one or two a week builds speed, accuracy and confidence without the fatigue of a full four-hour paper.

For video lessons, the official English with Cambridge YouTube channel is run by the exam board itself. It includes real C1 Advanced Speaking tests with examiner comments, so students can see exactly what the examiners are listening for.

About the C1 Advanced (CAE) exam

C1 Advanced, formerly the Certificate in Advanced English (CAE), is the Cambridge English qualification at level C1 on the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR). Passing it shows that a learner can use English confidently and flexibly for university study, professional work and everyday life in an English-speaking country. It's one level below C2 Proficiency and one level above B2 First.

The exam tests all four skills across four papers. You can take it on paper or on computer, and the certificate never expires.

What the exam is looking for

C1 Advanced isn't just a vocabulary test. It checks whether students can follow long and complex texts, spot attitude and opinion, paraphrase precisely, choose the right collocation or word form, organise an argument in writing, and keep a natural conversation going. The Use of English parts in particular reward accurate grammar and a wide range of vocabulary. That's why they're the focus of most of the mini tests on this page.

How students use it

  • University entry: universities in Australia, the UK, Europe and beyond accept it as proof of English for undergraduate and postgraduate courses.
  • Work and professional registration: employers and professional bodies use it as evidence of advanced, workplace-ready English.
  • Visas: it's accepted for some visa and migration applications, including some Australian visas. Always check the current requirements for your own visa.
  • A long-term goal: because the certificate doesn't expire, many advanced learners take it as lasting proof of their level, even without a deadline.

CAE, IELTS or PTE?

All three tests are widely accepted for study, work and migration, but they feel quite different on the day. The right one depends on where you're sending your results, and on how you like to be tested.

The skills overlap a lot. Paraphrasing, word formation, reading for detail and attitude, and writing a well-organised essay all count across all three tests. The themes and vocabulary in these mini tests carry straight over to IELTS and PTE preparation. If a student isn't sure which test to take, check exactly which ones their university, employer or visa accepts, and which score they need, before choosing.

Exam details are current as of November 2026. Test providers update their formats from time to time, so check the official Cambridge English, IELTS and Pearson websites before booking.
