Learn English with Music

Original songs turned into listening activities. Play the track, fill in what you hear, and check yourself as you go — nothing is timed, nothing is scored, and nothing asks you to sign up. Good on a phone alone, or on the board with a class.

The songs

Say It Like You Mean It — the ‑tion Rap
Theme: Rap · Level: B1–B2 · Word stress & intonation
How to use: a rap built on ‑tion, ‑sion, ‑ity and ‑ic words, where the suffix pulls the stress onto the syllable before it — education, possibility. Rap along with the beat and the stress lands in the right place on its own.
Suffix stress · Sentence rhythm
 I think about you — the Prepositions Song
Theme: Reggae · Level: A1–A2 · Elementary
How to use: a slow reggae beat with one preposition to the bar, so beginners have time to hear it and write it. Listen and fill in the missing preposition — place first, then time.
Place · Time · Movement
 Get Up, Stand Up by Bob Marley — Phrasal Verbs
Theme: Reggae · Level: A1–A2 · Elementary
How to use: a slow reggae beat with one preposition to the bar, so beginners have time to hear it and write it. Listen and fill in the missing preposition — place first, then time.
Place · Time · Movement
 Dance, Think, Flirt, Remember — All the Tenses
Theme: Latin pop · Level: B2–C1 · Listening gap-fill
How to use: one song, three languages, the same four verbs in every tense. Listen and type the English verbs into the gaps. The focus is when to use each tense, not only how to form it — the Spanish and Portuguese lines are there so you can see what your own language does at the same moment.
English · Español · Português
