Practice for PTE, IELTS and everyday life

Most importantly, they practise the conversations you actually have at work, in shops and with neighbours, so the language is useful long after the test.

How every situation works

Whatever the situation, a strong reply has the same four parts. Each practice page has a self-check list for these, a template if you get stuck, and a written guide with a model answer.

  • Introduction: who you are and why you are contacting them.
  • Key details: what happened, when, and why it matters.
  • Clear action: the fix, permission, advice or next step you want.
  • Polite closing: thanks, reassurance or an offer to help.

For PTE Respond to a Situation

In the test, the situation appears on screen and is played as audio. You have 10 seconds to prepare and 40 seconds to speak, and every page here uses the same timing. Content carries the most weight, followed by pronunciation and oral fluency, so aim to cover all four parts at a natural, steady pace rather than rushing to say more. Use Hide the text on any page to practise with the audio only, as in the real test.

For IELTS General Training Writing Task 1

The letter task asks you to write at least 150 words in about 20 minutes, covering three bullet points. The four parts above become your four paragraphs. Every practice page ends with three IELTS-style letter questions and a writing box with a word count. A good routine: answer a situation out loud first, then type your answer into the box and build it up into a full letter, adjusting the tone for a friend, a manager or a company.

For everyday life

Calling a shop about a broken parcel, telling your team about a roster change, or apologising to a neighbour are all easier when you have said something similar before. Short, regular practice builds the phrases you need, so they come to you when the real conversation happens.
