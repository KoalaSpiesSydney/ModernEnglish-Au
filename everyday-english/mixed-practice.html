One structure for every situation

A short guide, with a model answer for the first situation.

Every situation on this page uses the same four parts: say why you are contacting the person, give the key details, ask for a clear action, and close politely. What changes from one task type to the next is the third part, the action. Get that right and the rest of your answer falls into place.

Where you will use this

  • PTE Respond to a Situation. The situation appears on screen and is played as audio. You get 10 seconds to prepare and 40 seconds to speak. Content counts most, followed by pronunciation and oral fluency, and choosing language that suits the situation is part of content.
  • IELTS General Training Writing Task 1. You write a letter of at least 150 words in about 20 minutes, covering three bullet points. Every IELTS letter prompt uses one or more of these six functions, often several in the same letter.
  • Everyday life. Work, shops, landlords, friends and neighbours: almost every awkward conversation is one of these six types.

The six task types

  • Explaining: the action is what the change means for the listener, and what they need to do.
  • Complaining: the action is the specific fix you want, such as a replacement or a refund.
  • Asking for advice: the action is a direct question, such as "What would you do in my position?"
  • Requesting: the action is the exact permission you need, with dates, and how you will cover your work.
  • Suggesting: the action is your idea, plus the benefit it brings.
  • Apologising: the action is how you will put it right, and how you will stop it happening again.

Model answer: situation 1

The meeting that has moved forward. About 80 words, which fits comfortably in 40 seconds.

Hi everyone, sorry to interrupt, but I've got an important update. This morning's meeting has been moved forward by two hours, so it's now starting at eight instead of ten, in the same room. I know that's a big change at short notice, so if you've already got something booked at eight, please let our manager know straight away. If anyone needs the agenda before then, I can send it round in the next few minutes. Thanks, everyone, for being flexible.

Common mistakes

  • Using the same tone for every situation. Talking to a manager, a friend and a customer service agent all need slightly different language.
  • Describing the situation back to the listener instead of responding to it.
  • Leaving out the action, which is the part that changes most between task types.

IELTS General Training letter practice

Writing Task 1: spend about 20 minutes and write at least 150 words.

Turn what you have just practised into writing. Plan each letter with the same four parts as your spoken answer, one paragraph each, and match your tone to the opening line: Dear Sir or Madam for formal, a title and surname for semi-formal, and a first name for informal. You do not need to write any addresses.

Letter 1 Formal

Requesting. You would like to do a short professional course that runs for three days during working hours next month.

Write a letter to your manager. In your letter

  • describe the course
  • explain how it will help you in your job
  • say how your work will be covered while you are away

Begin your letter as follows: Dear Ms Lewis,

Letter 2 Informal

Asking for advice. You are planning to move to the city where an old friend lives, and you need help finding a place to live and a job.

Write a letter to your friend. In your letter

  • explain why you are planning to move
  • ask for advice about finding somewhere to live
  • ask what kind of work might suit you there

Begin your letter as follows: Dear …,

Letter 3 Formal

Suggesting. The park near your home is popular with families, but it has few facilities and is often untidy.

Write a letter to your local council. In your letter

  • describe the park and how people use it
  • explain what the problems are
  • suggest an improvement and how it would help residents

Begin your letter as follows: Dear Sir or Madam,

Everyday English Situations

Mixed Practice: instructions

Respond politely and clearly. Include:

  1. A short introduction saying why you are contacting the person.
  2. The key details of the problem, request or situation.
  3. A clear action you want (a solution, advice, permission or next steps).
  4. A polite closing showing appreciation or willingness to cooperate.

How to practise

Press Start. The situation is played once as a recorded audio prompt. After it finishes, you have 10 seconds to get ready, then a beep signals your 40-second response window. Use speech-to-text or type your answer. Press Repeat prompt to redo the same situation, or Next to move on.

Each situation has a different task type, shown in the coloured tag above it: explaining, complaining, asking for advice, requesting, suggesting or apologising. The four-part structure works for all of them.

Stuck for words? Open Useful language at the top of the page for ready-made phrases for each of the four parts.

Listening settings

The Listening settings button at the top right (Settings on a phone) opens a side panel where you control how the situation is played:

  • Playback speed: slow the recording down while you are learning, then bring it back up to normal speed.
  • Backup voice: if a recording cannot play on your device, the situation is read by your browser's built-in voice instead. Choose an Australian or British English voice to match the test.
  • Test backup voice: hear that voice before you start.

If speech-to-text is not picking up your voice, the panel also tells you what to check. In the real test you only hear the situation. To practise that way, tap Hide the text under the situation before you press Start.

One structure, six task types

Useful language for every situation

Opening

  • Hi [name], have you got a minute?
  • I'm calling about …
  • I just wanted to let you know that …
  • Sorry to bother you, but I need to talk to you about …

Giving the details

  • The thing is, … / What happened was …
  • Unfortunately, the … arrived damaged and doesn't work.
  • The meeting has been moved forward to …
  • I've been offered two jobs, and I can't decide between them.

Asking for action

  • Would it be possible to take two days off next week?
  • Could you send a replacement or give me a refund?
  • What would you do in my position?
  • I'd like to suggest a short meeting every morning.

Apologising and closing

  • I'm really sorry. It was my mistake.
  • To make sure it doesn't happen again, I'll …
  • Thanks so much for your understanding.
  • I'm happy to help in any way I can.
Help

Listening settings

Each situation plays as a recording. The backup voice is only used if a recording cannot play on your device. Choose an Australian or British English voice so it matches the accent used in the test. If the list is short, install extra voices in your device's language settings and reload the page.

If speech-to-text accuracy drops, check your browser microphone permissions and selected input device. Chrome and Edge give the best results.
