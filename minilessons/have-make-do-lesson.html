Have, Make or Do?

In English, some words go together. These pairs are called collocations.

We say do the dishes, not make the dishes.
We say make a mistake, not do a mistake.
We say have a shower, not make a shower.

There is no perfect rule – you learn the pairs one by one. But these three ideas help.

1 · have = it happens to you

HAVE meals, washing, rest, feeling ill, good times

You experience something. You do not make anything new.

  • have breakfast, have lunch, have dinner (eat a meal)
  • have a shower, have a bath
  • have a rest, have a break, have a nap
  • have a headache, have a cold
  • have fun, have a party, have a good time

Example: I have a shower every morning before work.

2 · make = create something new

MAKE food, plans, sounds, words

Before, there is nothing. After, there is something new.

  • make a cake, make a cup of tea (food and drink you prepare)
  • make a list, make a decision
  • make a mistake, make a mess, make noise
  • make a phone call, make a promise, make a joke

Test: is there something new at the end? → make

3 · do = work, jobs and activities

DO study, jobs at home, exercise

You do a job or an activity. You are busy, but you do not make a new thing.

  • do homework, do a course
  • do the dishes, do the shopping, do the laundry (jobs at home: often “do the …”)
  • do exercise, do yoga, do sport
  • do your best, do nothing

Test: is it a job or an activity? → do

Be careful: one word, two verbs

Some words go with two verbs, and the meaning changes.

  • make dinner = cook it  ·  have dinner = eat it
  • make friends = meet new friends  ·  have friends = they are your friends now
  • make a plan = think of it  ·  have a plan = you know what to do

  • have breakfast – eat your morning meal

  • have a rest – stop and relax for a while

  • have a nap – sleep for a short time during the day
  • have a dream – experience images and stories while sleeping
  • have a headache – feel pain in your head

  • have a temperature – be hotter than normal because you are ill
  • have a party – celebrate with friends and music
  • have a meeting – come together to talk about something
  • have a conversation – talk with another person
  • have a chat – talk in a friendly, informal way
  • have fun – enjoy yourself

  • have a look – use your eyes to see something

  • have a holiday – take time away from work or study

  • have a baby – give birth to a child
  • have a brother / a sister – talk about your family
  • have a pet – keep an animal at home
  • have an idea – think of something you could do
  • have a nightmare – have a frightening dream
  • have time – be free to do something

  • make breakfast – prepare the morning meal

  • make a cake – bake a cake

  • make a decision – choose what to do

Note for teachers: the game only uses words that go with one of the three verbs. Words like lunch, dinner, breakfast, coffee, money, a plan, friends, a sandwich, an appointment, nothing and a test work with more than one verb, so they are in these lists but not in the game.

Write from Dictation

Press ▶ Listen to hear the sentence. Type the missing expression using have, make, or do, then press Submit.

Voice:

1) I always ______ before I go to work.

Answer: have breakfast

2) Everyone ______ sometimes. Don't worry!

Answer: make a mistake (or: makes a mistake)

3) I ______ after dinner every evening.

Answer: do homework

4) I ______ every morning before work.

Answer: have a shower

5) It's hard to ______ when you have many options.

Answer: make a decision

6) Can you please ______ after dinner tonight?

Answer: do the dishes

7) I always ______ when I go to the beach.

Answer: have fun

8) It's easy to ______ when you join a club.

Answer: make friends

9) Don't worry about the test — just ______.

Answer: do your best
