Do, Does, Is or Are? – Mini Lesson and Practice

A short lesson on questions with do, does, is and are, then two exercises and speaking cards. You can also print it as a worksheet.

Is and Are, or Do and Does?

When you meet someone new, you ask a lot of questions. Listen to this:

“Where are you from? Do you live near here? What does your sister do? Is she a student too?”

Four questions, and each one needs a different word. How do you choose? Look for another verb in the question.

1 · no other verb

is / are + noun, adjective, age, place

The only verb is be.

  • Are you a student?
  • Is your sister married?
  • Where are you from?

Test: can you answer with am, is or are? → Yes, I am. ✓

2 · another verb

do / does + verb

There is another verb: live, work, have, like, speak …

  • Do you live near here?
  • Does your sister work?
  • Where do you work?

Test: can you answer with do or does? → Yes, I do. ✓

3 · one or more than one?

is / does or are / do

is and does go with he, she, it or one person or thing. are and do go with you, we, they or more than one.

  • Does your brother drive? · Do your brothers drive?
  • Is your teacher Australian? · Are your teachers Australian?

Remember: after does, the verb has no s: Does she work? (not works).

Also: common mistakes

Don't mix the two kinds of question.

  • Do you from Brazil? → Are you from Brazil?
  • Are you have a car? → Do you have a car?
  • Does she works here? → Does she work here?

Now practise: choose the word, fix the mistakes, then ask a partner.

Practice: Choose the Word

Tap do, does, is or are to finish each question.

    Practice: Fix the Mistake

    Each question has one wrong word. Type the question correctly.

    Speaking: Ask a Partner

    Say the question with the missing word. Then ask your partner. You can invent your answers.

    Listen to the answer, then ask one more question.

    Tell the Class

    Say three or four things about your partner. Remember the s: he lives, she speaks, he has.

    • My partner is from … and lives in …
    • She has two brothers. / He doesn't have any brothers or sisters.
    • His first language is … and he speaks …
    • Her hobbies are … and she likes …
