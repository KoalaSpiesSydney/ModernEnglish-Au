Get – Mini Lesson and Practice

A short lesson on three meanings of get, then two exercises. When you are ready, play the matching game.

Why Do We Say Get?

Native speakers use get to replace many words in the dictionary. Listen to this:

“Yesterday I got up, got dressed and got on the bus to get to work. I got there late, so my boss got worried.”

That is get six times! Each one replaces a different word:

  • got up = left my bed
  • got dressed = put on my clothes
  • got on the bus = entered the bus
  • get to work = arrive at work
  • got there = arrived there
  • got worried = became worried

How do you know what get means? Look at the word after it. Here are three common meanings.

1 · get = become

get + adjective

You change – your body, feelings or situation is different now.

  • I get hungry at 11 o'clock. (become hungry)
  • It's late. I'm getting tired.
  • She got lost in the city.

Test: can you say become? → become hungry ✓

2 · get = receive

get + a thing (noun)

Something comes to you, or you go and find it.

  • I got a message from Tom. (received)
  • Let's get some food. (go and find)
  • He gets paid on Friday.

Test: can you say receive or buy / find? → receive a message ✓

3 · get = arrive

get + a place

You come to a place. Your trip is finished.

  • What time do you get home? (arrive home)
  • I get to work at 9. (arrive at work)
  • We got there late. (arrived)

Remember: get to work / school / the park – but get home / here / there (no to).

Also: phrasal verbs

get + a small word (up, on, off, in, out) is a phrasal verb. The small word changes the meaning – often it shows how your body moves.

  • get up = leave your bed
  • get on / get off the bus, train, plane
  • get in / get out of the car, taxi

In the game, matched cards show which get it is: become, receive, arrive or phrasal verb.

Practice: Drag and Drop

What does get mean in each sentence? Drag the correct meaning on top of the blue word – or tap a meaning, then tap the blue words. You can use each meaning again and again.

Be careful with the tense! get is present. got is past.
