The Girl in the Graveyard

Once upon a time, on Halloween night, a young boy was walking home from school past the graveyard when he heard a bloodcurdling scream.

It was getting dark. Jack-o'-lanterns were grinning from every window, and children in costumes were running from door to door, shrieking “Trick or treat!” The boy didn't believe in superstitions, but he crossed his fingers anyway.

He stopped and turned towards the graveyard. A thick fog was creeping between the crumbling headstones. He was peering at the oldest headstone when he felt a hand upon his shoulder from behind. His heart was pounding. He turned around and glimpsed a small girl in a white dress with long black hair covering her face.

She was clutching a black cat. “Trick or treat, my lovely,” she said, in the voice of a very old lady. Then she let out a wicked cackle and vanished into the graveyard. A shiver ran down the boy's spine. He remembered his grandmother's old warning: a woman's scream in the night means that someone is going to die.

He kept walking, faster now. But while he was hurrying past the church, he heard her scream again. The full moon was rising over the trees. Then he heard a howl.

    Past simple or past continuous?

    He was walking home… when he heard a scream.

    A story needs two kinds of past verbs. The past continuous paints the picture: what was happening around the boy. The past simple tells us what happened next.

    Past simple

    verb + -ed, or an irregular form

    • A finished action in the past.
      He crossed his fingers.
    • Actions one after another. They move the story forward.
      He stopped and turned towards the graveyard.
      She let out a wicked cackle and vanished.
    • A short action that interrupts a longer one.
      He was peering at the headstone when he felt a hand on his shoulder.
    Past continuous

    was / were + verb-ing

    • An action in progress at a moment in the past.
      He was walking home from school.
    • The scene: what was happening around the story.
      It was getting dark. A thick fog was creeping between the headstones.
    • A longer action that is interrupted.
      He was peering at the headstone when he felt a hand on his shoulder.

    The main action and the background

    The past simple is the main action. It drives the story forward: something happens, then something else happens.

    The past continuous is the background. The action is unfinished: it is still going on, and we are waiting for the main action in the past simple.

    A vampire jumped in front of me.This is the main action. It happened, it's finished, and it's important.
    A vampire was jumping out at me…This sounds like the background. It isn't finished, and we are waiting for something more important… when I woke up!

    Look at the end of the story: The full moon was rising over the trees. The moon is still rising, so we wait for the main action… Then he heard a howl.

    Teacher Notes / Answer Key

    Suggested lesson (about 60 minutes)

    1. Warm-up: play the Halloween Matching Game in teams, so the story vocabulary is fresh (10–15 min).
    2. Pre-teach the vocabulary: students tap each word to hear it, then test each other in pairs (one reads a meaning, the other says the word) (5–10 min).
    3. Read and listen to the story (10 min).
    4. Comprehension. Ask students to point to the evidence in the story (10 min). Questions with was / were … doing need a past continuous answer; questions with did … do need the past simple. Question 7 is an inference question: the story never names the banshee, so let pairs argue for their answer before revealing it.
    5. Grammar: press Show the grammar and ask what the orange verbs have in common before showing the board. (10 min).
    6. Gap fill, alone then checked in pairs (10 min).
    7. Extra practice for students who need it: English Grammar in Use (intermediate, 5th edition), Units 5 and 6.
    8. Homework: students extend the story. Next lesson they read their endings aloud and the class votes for the scariest.

    Answers

    A model ending

    Then he heard a howl. It was coming from the trees behind the church. The boy was running towards the gate when he saw a huge werewolf. Its eyes were glowing red in the moonlight. He couldn't move. While the werewolf was creeping towards him, he heard a wicked cackle. The little girl was standing between the headstones, and she was clutching her black cat. “Leave him alone,” she said in her old lady's voice. The werewolf let out one last howl and vanished into the fog. When the boy looked down, there was a sweet in his pocket. He never walked past the graveyard on Halloween night again.

    Common mistakes to watch for

    • Using the past continuous for every verb (“He was turning and was seeing…”). The events need the past simple.
    • Stative verbs in the continuous: was knowing, was believing, was wanting.
    • Was / were agreement: they was running → they were running; everyone were dancing → everyone was dancing.
    • Spelling: running, stopping (double the consonant), rising, shining (drop the e), hurrying (keep the y).
