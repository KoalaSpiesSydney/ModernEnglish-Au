Relative Clauses with Where – Mini Lesson and Practice

A short lesson on where in relative clauses, and when a place needs which or that instead. Then two exercises and speaking cards. You can also print it as a worksheet.

Where, Which or That?

After a place word, you can't always use where. Listen to this:

“This is the town where I grew up. It's a town which most tourists never visit. That's the café where I had my first job, and that's the park that I loved as a child.”

Town, café and park are all places, but only two of them take where. The choice depends on what happens inside the clause.

1 · something happens there

place + where + subject + verb

The clause is already complete. The place is only where it happens.

  • This is the town where I grew up. (I grew up there)
  • We found a beach where nobody was swimming.
  • Is there a shop where I can buy stamps?

Test: can you add there? → I grew up there. ✓

2 · the place is the subject or object

place + which / that

The place does something, or someone visits, likes or sees it.

  • It's a town which attracts tourists. (it attracts)
  • The museum which we visited was free. (we visited it)
  • That's the café that I told you about.

Test: can you add it? → We visited it. ✓ So not where.

3 · prepositions

where = in, at or on which

Where already includes the preposition, so don't say it twice.

  • the house where I live ✓
  • the house which I live in ✓
  • the house in which I live ✓ (formal)

Not: the house where I live in

Also: where without a real place

Where also follows words like situation, case, point and stage.

  • a situation where nobody wins
  • cases where the rule doesn't apply
  • We reached a point where we had to stop.

Now practise: choose the word, join the sentences, then describe the places in your life.

Practice: Where or Which?

Tap where or which to finish each sentence. Use the test: can you add there, or it?

    Practice: Join the Sentences

    Make one sentence with a relative clause. Use where, which or that.

    Speaking: Places in Your Life

    Say the missing word, then answer. Describe the place so your partner can imagine it.

    Answer for one minute, then your partner asks one more question.

    Tell the Class

    Report three things your partner said. Use where at least once, and which or that at least once.

    • Sam told me about the café where he studies every weekend.
    • The city that Li would love to visit is …
    • Noor grew up in a town which has only one school.
