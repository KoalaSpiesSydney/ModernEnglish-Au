How to play

♥ Lovers vs ✕ Haters

  1. Split into two teams: the Lovers and the Haters. The Lovers go first.
  2. On your turn, flip two cards – a dark word card and a light meaning card.
  3. A match? Win the points and go again. No match? It's the other team's turn.
  4. When every card is matched, the team with the most points wins the round. Scores carry over to the next round.

Lovers & Haters – Matching Game

Workaholics, clean freaks, technophobes and bibliophiles – English has a word for people who love, hate, fear or just can't stop. Two teams, the Lovers and the Haters, take turns to match each word with its meaning. Harder words win more points.

Mini Lesson: Word Parts for Lovers, Haters and People Who Can't Stop

Look at the end (or start) of the word – it usually tells you whether the person loves, hates, fears or can't stop.

♥ Loves

-phile
loves a thing or culture (bibliophile, Francophile)
-ie
an enthusiast, informal (foodie)
-ist
believes in or is devoted to (minimalist, pacifist, environmentalist)

✕ Hates or fears

-phobe
fears or strongly dislikes (arachnophobe, technophobe)
mis-
hates, from Greek (misogynist = women, misandrist = men, misanthrope = people)
-ist
also judges unfairly (racist, sexist, ageist)

↻ Can't stop

-aholic
addicted, often jokey (workaholic, chocoholic)
freak
obsessed, informal (clean freak, control freak)
-maniac
a compulsion, clinical (kleptomaniac, pyromaniac)

Opposites: technophobe ↔ technophile · xenophobe ↔ xenophile · minimalist ↔ maximalist · realist ↔ idealist.
Expert roots: turo- cheese · oeno- wine · pluvio- rain · ailuro- cat · selen- moon · coulro- clown · ergo- work · triskaideka- thirteen.
Careful: -aholic and freak are usually light-hearted; -ist words like racist and sexist, and homophobe, are serious criticisms.

Speaking: IELTS Part 1 Style

These are the kinds of questions that are common in IELTS Speaking Part 1, where the examiner asks short questions about you and your everyday life. Ask a partner – or a friend or family member – and answer using the words from the game. Precise, less common words like bibliophile or clean freak show a wide vocabulary, which helps your score for Lexical Resource – one of the four things the examiner marks. The words under each question will help.

1 · AskStudent A is the examiner and reads (or plays 🔈) each question.
2 · AnswerStudent B answers in 2–3 sentences using a word from the game: answer + reason + example.
3 · SwapChange roles for the next topic – or ask someone you know and tell the class what they said.
