PTE Speaking · Respond to a Situation

Explaining: instructions

Respond politely and clearly. Include:

  1. A short introduction saying what you are explaining and why you are contacting them.
  2. The key facts in a sensible order: what happened, when, and why.
  3. What it means for the listener: what they need to do, or what to expect.
  4. A check that they have followed you, and a polite closing.

How to practise

Press Start. The situation is read aloud once by the built-in voice, so no audio files are needed. After it finishes, you have 10 seconds to get ready, then a beep signals your 40-second response window. Use speech-to-text or type your answer. Press Repeat prompt to redo the same situation, or Next to move on.

Stuck for words? Open Useful language at the top of the page for ready-made phrases for each of the four parts.

Listening settings

The Listening settings button at the top right (Settings on a phone) opens a side panel where you control the voice that reads the situation:

  • Prompt voice: choose an Australian or British English voice, to match the accent used in the test.
  • Speaking speed: slow the voice down while you are learning, then bring it back up to normal speed.
  • Test voice: hear the voice before you start.

If speech-to-text is not picking up your voice, the panel also tells you what to check. In the real test you only hear the situation. To practise that way, tap Hide the text under the situation before you press Start.

Clear and ordered: the tone examiners expect

Useful language for explaining

Starting off

  • I'm calling to let you know about a change to tomorrow.
  • I'll quickly run you through how it works.
  • There are two things you need to know.
  • Let me explain what happened.

Ordering the facts

  • First… then… after that…
  • The reason for the delay is that the warehouse ran out of stock.
  • It started on Saturday night, and by the morning…
  • The important part is the start time.

What it means for them

  • So for you, that means arriving an hour earlier.
  • You don't need to do anything — I've already booked it.
  • What that means in practice is a wait until Tuesday.
  • All you'll need to do is get off at the second stop.

Checking and closing

  • Does that make sense so far?
  • Let me know if you'd like me to go over any of it again.
  • I'll send it in writing as well, so you have it.
  • Thanks for your patience with this.
Help

Listening settings

Choose an Australian or British English voice so the listening practice matches the accent used in the test. If the list is short, install extra voices in your device's language settings and reload the page.

If speech-to-text accuracy drops, check your browser microphone permissions and selected input device. Chrome and Edge give the best results.
