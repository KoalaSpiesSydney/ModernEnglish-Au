Why tone matters

The same idea can sound chatty or professional. Choosing the right tone (or register) is marked in exams and noticed at work – a casual phrase in an essay or report can cost you marks or make you sound less expert.

IELTS WritingPTE AcademicAcademic EnglishBusiness English
CasualThe prices went up a lot.
AcademicThe costs increased significantly.

  • Avoid contractions – it is, not it's.
  • Choose one formal verb over a phrasal verb – increase, not go up.

Mini lesson: five ways to make your English sound academic

Academic English is not about using the longest words you know. It is about being precise, objective and careful with your claims. Every pair in the game uses one or more of these five techniques.

1 · Choose precise words over vague ones

Casual English uses general words like a lot, big, good and things. Academic writing chooses a word that says exactly how much, how important or what kind.

CasualAcademic
a lot / very bigconsiderable, substantial, enormous, significant(ly)
things, partsfactors, aspects, elements
goodbeneficial, effective, encouraging
badineffective, harmful, detrimental
tiny mistakesminor errors

2 · Use one formal verb instead of a phrasal verb

Phrasal verbs are normal in speech, but academic writing often prefers a single verb that comes from Latin. The prices went up becomes The costs increased.

CasualAcademic
go up / go downincrease, rise / decrease, decline
find outdiscover, establish
look intoinvestigate, examine
keep going, carry oncontinue, maintain
come up withdevise, propose
get betterimprove

Not every phrasal verb is too casual. Carry out, consist of, account for and result in are common in academic writing.

3 · Make the idea the subject, not the people

Casual English puts people first: everyone, they, people. Academic English often puts the idea first with It is…, There is… or the passive. This sounds more objective.

  • Everyone knows that… → It is common knowledge that…
  • People think it's a bad idea. → It is widely regarded as ineffective.
  • They agree. → There is common ground.
  • There is still a big argument about that. → Considerable debate continues.

4 · Be careful with your claims (hedging)

Academic writers rarely say something is 100% certain. They use hedging language to show how strong the evidence is. Compare The first results look good with Preliminary findings seem encouraging: the academic version tells the reader the results are early and the conclusion is not final.

  • Verbs: seem, appear, suggest, indicate, tend to
  • Modals: may, might, could
  • Adverbs and adjectives: likely, possibly, arguably, largely

Avoid This proves that… unless it really does. This suggests that… is usually safer.

5 · Drop the chat

Some features belong to conversation, not to essays and reports:

  • Contractions: write it is, do not, cannot – not it's, don't, can't.
  • Slang and informal words: for ages, loads of, stuff, kids, uni → for an extended period, a large number of, material, children, university.
  • Intensifiers: really, so, pretty, super – choose a stronger word instead (really important → crucial).
  • Questions to the reader and exclamation marks.

Useful academic collocations from the game

Learn these as chunks – the words naturally go together:

  • wield influence / power / authority
  • undertake research / a study / a project
  • attract support / criticism / attention
  • play a pivotal / crucial / key role in
  • maintain engagement / standards / a program
  • preliminary findings / results; conflicting evidence / findings

Don't overdo it. Utilise is not better than use, and a sentence full of rare words is hard to read. In IELTS Writing, the Lexical Resource score rewards vocabulary that is precise and used with awareness of style and collocation – not just long words.

Rewrite practice: casual to academic

Rewrite each casual sentence in academic English. Use the five techniques from the mini lesson, then open the model answer to compare. There is more than one correct answer.

  1. Loads of people use their phones on the train.
    Model answer

    A substantial number of commuters use mobile phones while travelling.

  2. The government is looking into the problem.
    Model answer

    The government is investigating the issue.

  3. Prices went down a bit last year.
    Model answer

    Prices decreased slightly in the previous year.

  4. Scientists found out that the drug doesn't really work.
    Model answer

    Researchers discovered that the drug is largely ineffective.

  5. Everybody thinks uni is really important these days.
    Model answer

    University education is widely regarded as essential today.

  6. Kids who read a lot do better at school.
    Model answer

    Children who read extensively tend to achieve higher academic results.

Teacher notes: a 45-minute lesson

Level: C1–C2 (strong B2 students can do the pre-teaching and game). Useful for: IELTS Writing Task 2, PTE Academic, university preparation and business report writing.

  1. Warm-up (5 min). Write The prices went up a lot and The costs increased significantly on the board. Ask: where would you hear or read each one? What exactly is different?
  2. Pre-teach (10 min). Open Pre-teach: key words. Students mark the words they already know, then teach each other the rest. Drill the stress change in conFLICT / CONflict.
  3. Play (10–15 min). Students play solo on their phones (use the QR button) or in teams on the board. Every time a team finds a pair, they read both sentences aloud and say which technique it uses.
  4. Gap-fill (10 min). Individually, then compare answers in pairs before checking.
  5. Rewrite (10 min). Students rewrite the six sentences, then compare with the model answers. For homework, they take a paragraph from their own writing and make it more academic.

Common questions

Is academic English the same as formal English? Academic English is a type of formal English with extra features: cautious claims, references to evidence and an impersonal style. A formal business email can still say we and please; an academic essay usually focuses on ideas and evidence.

Can students use "I" in academic writing? It depends on the task. IELTS Writing Task 2 often asks for your opinion, so I believe is fine in moderation. Many university subjects, especially in the sciences, prefer an impersonal style.
