Pre-teach: the six words

These six words share the letters -ough, but each one sounds different and does a different job. Say each word aloud before you play – the sound is the best clue to the meaning.

Sound, meaning and an example

thoughtrhymes with sport
the past of think, or a noun: an ideaI thought you were Japanese. · That's a good thought.
thoughrhymes with go
joins two contrasting ideas, like althoughEven though it was raining, we went out.
throughrhymes with blue
in one side and out the other; from A to BWe drove through the tunnel.
thoroughTHUH-ruh – two syllables
adjective: complete and carefulThe police did a thorough search.
throughoutthrough + OUT
in every part of a place or a timeIt rained throughout the night.
thoroughlyTHUH-ruh-lee
adverb: completely, carefullyWash your hands thoroughly.

Four tricks to remember the spelling

thoughTmemory trick
ends in T, like think: a thought is something you thinkI thought about it.
thoRoughmemory trick
has an extra O, so it takes longer: thorough work is careful worka thorough check
throughOUTmemory trick
through + out: all the way, from start to endthroughout the year
thoughmemory trick
the shortest one, and it sounds like so – both join ideasIt's late, though.

Why -ough is so confusing

English spelling stopped changing hundreds of years ago, but pronunciation kept moving. The letters -ough were once pronounced with a sound like the ch in Scottish loch. Over time that sound disappeared or changed in different ways in different words – so today the same four letters can be read in at least seven ways.

So when you meet a new -ough word, don't guess from the spelling – listen to it or check a dictionary.

Though, although or even though?

  • Although and though mean the same at the start of a clause: Although / Though it was cold, we swam. Though is a little more informal.
  • Even though is stronger – it shows real surprise: Even though she was sick, she finished the race.
  • Only though can go at the end of a sentence, where it means however: It's expensive. It's beautiful, though.

What about "thru"?

Thru is an informal American spelling you may see on road signs (drive-thru) or in text messages. Don't use it in essays, exams or emails at work – always write through.

Practice

Which word fills the gap?

Choose thought, though, through, thorough, throughout or thoroughly. Say your answer, then check.

  1. She read the contract ______ before she signed it.
    Show answer

    thoroughly – adverb: completely and carefully

  2. ______ I'd studied hard, the test was really difficult.
    Show answer

    Though (or Even though / Although) – it contrasts two ideas

  3. It rained ______ the whole holiday.
    Show answer

    throughout – from the start to the end of a time

  4. I ______ about you all day.
    Show answer

    thought – the past of think

  5. Drive ______ the town and turn left at the bridge.
    Show answer

    through – in one side and out the other

  6. The doctor gave me a ______ check-up.
    Show answer

    thorough – adjective before a noun: complete and careful

  7. Nice idea. I'm not sure it will work, ______.
    Show answer

    though – at the end of a sentence it means however

  8. Give it some ______ before you decide.
    Show answer

    thought – a noun: careful thinking

Fix the mistake

These are real mistakes that students – and native speakers – make. Find the wrong word and correct it.

  1. I through it was Tuesday.
    Show answer

    I thought it was Tuesday. – past of think

  2. Even thought it was late, we kept talking.
    Show answer

    Even though it was late… – even though joins two ideas

  3. We drove thorough the night.
    Show answer

    We drove through the night. – from the start to the end

  4. She's very through – she checks everything twice.
    Show answer

    She's very thorough. – an adjective: careful

For teachers: lesson notes

Using the game in class

Level: B1–B2. Strong A2 groups can manage with the pre-teach cards. Time: 30–45 minutes with the mini lesson.

  1. Pronunciation first (5 min). Write the four words on the board. Students say them aloud and match each to a rhyme: go, blue, sport, THUH-ruh. Most mistakes in this set start with the sound.
  2. Pre-teach (5–10 min). Open Pre-teach: the six words from the How to Play card, or teach the full mini lesson first.
  3. Play (10–15 min). Teams take turns. The ninth tile has no partner: when the round ends, both teams write their answer for it, and the team that's right gets a bonus point.
  4. Practice (10 min). The gap-fills, then Fix the mistake in pairs.

Extension ideas

  • -ough dictation: dictate a sentence with four -ough words, such as Although the dough was tough, I thought it was enough. Students write it, then group the words by sound.
  • Spot the -ough: students find three -ough words in a news article and sort them using the sound table above.
  • Through idioms (B2): sleep through, see through someone, go through a bag, be through with someone – students write a short dialogue using two of them.
