How to play

1, 2 or 3 players
  1. It works like Memory (also called Pairs), the matching card game that children often play. All the tiles start face down.
  2. Turn over two tiles. If you have found an idiom and its matching meaning, they stay face up and the pair is yours — have another turn.
  3. If they do not match, they turn back over and it is the next player’s turn.
  4. Play until every pair has been found.

Keep your own score. The counter on the board shows how many pairs have been found altogether, not how many each player has won — so remember your own total. Most pairs at the end wins.

Mini lesson: what colours mean in English idioms

In English, each colour carries a few ideas: red is anger and danger, green is plants, envy and feeling sick, blue is sadness. Once you know what a colour means, most colour idioms make sense. Here are all 30 idioms from Levels 1–3, sorted by colour, with the reason behind each one.

Red

  • anger
  • blood
  • love and passion
  • danger and warnings
  • special occasions
  • official rules
  • Level 2See red – become suddenly very angryRed is the colour of anger: a red face, blood rising.
  • Level 1Catch someone red-handed – catch someone in the act of doing wrongCaught with blood still on their hands.
  • Level 1Roll out the red carpet – give someone a special welcomeA red carpet is laid for kings, queens and film stars.
  • Level 3Paint the town red – go out and celebrate in a lively wayRed is the colour of excitement and energy.
  • Level 3A red-letter day – a day that is important and worth rememberingOld calendars printed festivals and holy days in red ink.
  • Level 2Red tape – official rules that slow everything downGovernment papers were once tied together with red ribbon.
  • Level 3A red herring – a detail that distracts from the real issueA smoked fish with a strong smell. The story goes that it was dragged across a trail to confuse hunting dogs.

More with red: in the red – owing money; banks once wrote debts in red ink · a red flag – a warning sign that something is wrong

Green

  • plants and nature
  • go
  • envy and jealousy
  • feeling sick
  • new and inexperienced
  • Level 1Give the green light – give permission to beginGreen means go on traffic lights.
  • Level 2Have green fingers – be very good at growing plantsA good gardener's hands are green from plants. In American English: a green thumb.
  • Level 1Green with envy – very jealous of someoneShakespeare called jealousy “the green-eyed monster”.
  • Level 3Green around the gills – looking as though you are about to be sickA sick person's face can look pale and greenish. “Gills” is a joke word for the face.

More with green: be green – be new to something and lack experience · go green – start living in a way that helps the environment

Blue

  • sadness
  • the sky
  • rare events
  • workers' clothes
  • noble families
  • Level 1Feel blue – feel sad or lowBlue is the colour of sadness, as in blues music.
  • Level 1Out of the blue – suddenly and without warningLike lightning from a clear blue sky.
  • Level 1Once in a blue moon – very rarelyA “blue moon” is a second full moon in one month, which is rare.
  • Level 2A blue-collar job – work that involves manual labourFactory and building workers often wore blue shirts or overalls.
  • Level 3Blue blood – noble or aristocratic family backgroundRich families who never worked outside had pale skin, so their veins looked blue.

More with blue: true blue – Australian English: loyal and genuine

Black

  • bad or unwanted
  • illegal or secret
  • clear, printed words

  • Level 2The black sheep – the family member seen as the odd one outBlack wool was worth less than white wool.
  • Level 2Blacklist someone – put someone on a list of people to avoidA list of bad or unwanted people.
  • Level 3The black market – illegal buying and selling of goodsTrade that happens in the dark, out of sight of the law.

More with white: as white as a sheet – very pale from fear or shock
