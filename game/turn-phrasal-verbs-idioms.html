For teachers: lesson plan

A 60-minute lesson

Levels: Level 1 (A2–B1) teaches literal uses like turn the TV down. Level 2 (B1–B2) teaches phrasal verbs with new meanings like turn down a job and it turned out. Level 3 (C1) teaches idioms like turn a blind eye and turn the tables. Useful for: phrasal verbs, storytelling and IELTS Speaking Part 2.

  1. Warm-up (5 min). Mime with the classroom TV or speaker: What am I doing? Elicit turn it on, turn it up, turn it down, turn it off.
  2. Pre-teach (10 min). Students do Step 1: Guess the meaning in pairs, then check the word list. Point out word order: turn it down ✓ turn down it ✗.
  3. Play (15 min). Project the board and split the class into two teams. Before a team keeps a card, a player must read the whole sentence aloud. With phones, share the QR code and play in pairs.
  4. Practice (10 min). Gap-fill and word order for the level, then check with a partner.
  5. Rewrite the story (15 min). Pairs rewrite the story, then one student reads the new version aloud while the class listens for each turn phrase.
  6. Speaking (5 min). Ask: Have you ever turned down a good offer? What turns you off a restaurant? Tell me about a day that turned out better than you expected. Is it ever OK to turn a blind eye?

Extension ideas

  • Noun forms: turnout, turnover, turnaround, a turn-off. Students write a news headline with each.
  • Opposites: turn on / turn off also mean attract / make someone lose interest (informal: Smoking is a real turn-off.). Use your judgement with younger classes.
  • Australian English: people often say switch on / switch off for lights, and rock up (informal) for turn up.

  4. If it doesn't, the card turns back over. Remember where it was!
