  • Have you ever had to tighten your belt? What did you stop spending money on?

  • Who should foot the bill for a wedding: the couple or their families?

  • Did your parents ever tell you that money doesn't grow on trees? What did they mean?

10 money idioms: meanings and examples

Use this list to check your answers, or study it before you play.

Break the bank
To use up all your money; to cost more than you can afford. Often used in the negative.
A weekend in the Blue Mountains won't break the bank if you take the train.
Tighten your belt
To spend less because you have less money.
After our rent went up, we had to tighten our belts and stop eating out.
Cost an arm and a leg
To be extremely expensive.
Parking in the Sydney CBD can cost an arm and a leg.
In the red
Owing money; having a negative bank balance or making a loss.
The café was in the red for its first six months.
In the black
Making a profit; having a positive balance.
By Christmas, the business was finally in the black.
Money doesn't grow on trees
Money is limited, so you shouldn't waste it.
Turn the air con off when you go out. Money doesn't grow on trees!
Pay through the nose
To pay much more than something is really worth.
We paid through the nose for flights during the school holidays.
Rolling in money
Very rich. You can also say rolling in it.
She bought a harbourside apartment, so she must be rolling in money.
On the house
Free, because the restaurant, bar or business pays for it.
Our meal was late, so the waiter brought us dessert on the house.
Foot the bill
To pay for something, often for everyone or when the cost is large.
The company footed the bill for the end-of-year party.

Teacher notes

Level: B2 (upper intermediate). Time: 25–35 minutes. You need: student phones or laptops with headphones. Tap QR at the top of the page to show a code students can scan.

  1. Match (10 min): students play in pairs for two or three rounds, so they see all 10 idioms.
  2. Dictation (10 min): students work alone and replay each recording as many times as they need. The checker ignores capital letters but counts every word, so they're and they are are marked differently.
  3. Discussion (10–15 min): pairs ask each other the five questions and then the extra questions. Ask a few pairs to report back.
  4. Extension: each student writes a new discussion question using an idiom that wasn't in the dictation, then asks the class.
