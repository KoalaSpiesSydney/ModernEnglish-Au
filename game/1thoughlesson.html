The Lesson – Four Words That Look the Same

Four words that look the same ·

1. The four words
thought /θɔːt/ – one syllable. It rhymes with bought, sport, caught.

The past of think — or a noun meaning an idea.

  • I thought the shop closed at 6pm. (past of think)
  • She thought about it for a week. (past of think)
  • That's an interesting thought. (noun = idea)
  • What are your thoughts on the plan? (plural noun)
Test it: can you replace it with believed or idea? Then it's thought.
though /ðəʊ/ – one syllable. It rhymes with go, no, slow.

Despite / although / but. It contrasts two parts of a sentence.

  • Though it was raining, we went to the beach.
  • Even though he lied, people believed him.
  • It's expensive. It's good quality, though. (at the end = however)
Test it: can you replace it with although or however? Then it's though. It is the only one of the four that joins two ideas.
through /θruː/ – one syllable. It rhymes with blue, do, new.

In one side and out the other. From A to B. In idioms, it can also mean finished.

  • The train went through the tunnel. (in and out)
  • We walked through the park to the station. (A to B)
  • I'm through with you. It's over. (finished)
  • I went through my bag but couldn't find my keys. (searched it)
  • I can see through his lies. (I understand the truth)
Test it: can you draw an arrow going in one side and out the other? Then it's through.
thorough /ˈθʌrə/ – TWO syllables: THUH-ruh. This is the only long one.

Complete and careful — nothing is missed. It is an adjective.

  • You've done a thorough job. (nothing was left out)
  • The police made a thorough search of the house.
  • She's very thorough. She checks every detail.
  • You've made a thorough mess! (a complete one — it can be negative too)
Test it: can you replace it with complete or careful? Then it's thorough. If you can hear two syllables, it's this word.
2. Two extra family members
throughout /θruːˈaʊt/ – through + out.

In every part of a place, or from the beginning to the end of a period of time.

  • Cockatoos live throughout Australia. (in every part of it)
  • Throughout his life, he exercised every morning. (start to finish)
  • It rained throughout the night.
thoroughly /ˈθʌrəli/ – three syllables: THUH-ruh-lee.

The adverb of thorough = completely, properly.

  • Wash your hands thoroughly.
  • I thoroughly enjoyed that film.
  • I was thoroughly confused by the instructions.

4. Spot the difference

We walked through the park.

We walked throughout the park.

The first one: we entered on one side and left on the other. The second one: we covered every part of it.

I thought it was closed.

Though it was closed, we waited.

The first is a verb (past of think). The second joins two contrasting ideas.

She read through the report.

She gave the report a thorough read.

Same idea, different grammar: through follows the verb; thorough describes the noun.

I'm through with this job.

I'm thorough with this job.

The first means I have finished. The second means I am careful and complete. One letter, a very different meeting with your boss.
