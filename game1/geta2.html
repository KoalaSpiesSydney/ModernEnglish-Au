Mini Lesson: Three Ways to Use Get

Get is one of the most common verbs in English. Look at the word after it – that tells you what get means.

1 · get = become

get + adjective

You change – your body, feelings or situation is different now.

  • I get hungry at 11 o'clock. (become hungry)
  • It's late. I'm getting tired.
  • She got lost in the city.

Test: can you say become? → become hungry ✓

2 · get = receive / obtain

get + a thing (noun)

Something comes to you, or you go and find it.

  • I got a message from Tom. (received)
  • Let's get some food. (go and find)
  • He gets paid on Friday.

Test: can you say receive or buy / find? → receive a message ✓

3 · phrasal verbs

get + up / on / off / in / out / to

Your body moves or you arrive somewhere. The small word shows the direction.

  • I get up at 7. (out of bed)
  • Get on the bus. Get off at the park.
  • What time do you get home? (arrive)

Remember: bus, train, plane → on / off · car, taxi → in / out of
