Teacher Notes / Full Idiom List (B2–C1)

An idiom is a fixed phrase whose meaning is different from the meaning of its words: nobody really puts a cat in a bag! Unlike the similes in the A1–B1 games, idioms don't use like or as … as, so learners have to know them as whole chunks.

B2 – Upper-intermediate

  • let the cat out of the bag – reveal a secret
  • the lion's share – the biggest part
  • a wolf in sheep's clothing – someone who seems kind but is dangerous
  • hold your horses – wait; don't rush
  • smell a rat – suspect something is wrong
  • get your ducks in a row – get organised
  • kill two birds with one stone – achieve two things with one action
  • monkey business – silly or dishonest behaviour
  • a dark horse – someone who surprises everyone by succeeding
  • eagle-eyed – very good at noticing details

C1 – Advanced

  • a sacred cow – something people refuse to criticise
  • the elephant in the room – an obvious problem nobody wants to discuss
  • a paper tiger – something that seems powerful but is weak
  • let sleeping dogs lie – don't bring up old problems again
  • put the cat among the pigeons – cause trouble or controversy
  • a wild goose chase – a pointless search for something
  • ride the tiger – be stuck in a risky situation you can't easily leave
  • birds of a feather flock together – similar people like to be together
  • a fly in the ointment – a small problem that spoils something good
  • stir up a hornet's nest – cause a lot of anger and trouble

Usage notes: the verbs inside idioms change form (she let the cat out of the bag; I smelt/smelled a rat) and possessives change (get our ducks in a row). Birds of a feather flock together is often shortened to birds of a feather. Put the cat among the pigeons is mainly British and Australian English.

Complete the Sentences

Complete each sentence with the right animal idiom. Change the verb or pronoun if the sentence needs it, then press Submit.

1) It was meant to be a surprise party, but my little brother ______ at breakfast.

Answer: let the cat out of the bag

2) The new manager seemed so friendly at first, but he turned out to be ______.

Answer: a wolf in sheep's clothing

3) Everyone knew the company was losing money, but nobody mentioned it. It was ______.

Answer: the elephant in the room

4) When the email asked for my bank password to claim a prize, I immediately ______.

Answer: smelt a rat / smelled a rat

5) If I cycle to work, I'll ______: I'll save money and get fit.

Answer: kill two birds with one stone

6) ______! We can't sign the contract until the lawyer has read it.

Answer: hold your horses

7) The inspectors arrive on Monday, so we need to ______ before then.

Answer: get our ducks in a row

8) The shop said the part was in stock, but it wasn't there. It was ______.

Answer: a wild goose chase
