ELTS Reading Changes in 2026: Fewer TFNG, More Matching Tasks — What the Experts Are Saying

The IELTS Reading test is quietly evolving in 2026. While the core format remains the same, some have speculated that there is a subtle shift in question-type frequency:

In particular, what they are inferring it that:

  • Fewer True/False/Not Given (TFNG) questions
  • More Matching Sentence Endings and other matching tasks

Although IELTS, Cambridge and IDP have not issued a single global announcement, recent test versions show a reduction in TFNG questions. This is consistent with:

  • IDP’s emphasis on paraphrasing and meaning-based comprehension which comes in reaction to Pearson putting an emphasis on such skills.
  • The increasing use of matching tasks that test logic, cohesion, and idea relationships
  • The computer-delivered interface, which favours side-by-side matching tasks and just looks neater that the TFNG.

So, according to some pundits and totally unofficial, TFNG remains part of the test — but it is no longer the dominant question type in many 2025–2026 test papers.

What TFNG is about anyway?

According to LizIELTS TFNG requires extremely precise meaning comparison. She emphasises that TFNG questions are not about keywords, but about exact meaning — a skill many candidates struggle with.

Her full TFNG guide: 🔗 IELTS Liz – True/False/Not Given Guide

She repeatedly warns that TFNG questions take longer because candidates must compare statements line-by-line with the passage. This probably the main complaint that Cambridge gets about the question style. People with otherwise solid skills waste time look for keywords and decoding a logic that actually isn’t at all logical to native speakers (it wasn’t to mean when I taught and I came to understand there is academic reasoning in its design, working out reasoning within the language not just memorisaton of vocabulary).

Liz explains that the most common error is mistaking “Not Given” for “False,” which leads to lower accuracy. This the illogical part- natives fail it because they don’t know the logic and people who aren’t that great in English can deduce it because they have learned the logic.

This is one reason IELTS may be reducing TFNG frequency in favour of tasks that test broader comprehension.

2. The Rise of Matching Sentence Endings

Matching Sentence Endings is becoming more common in 2026 test versions. This task type requires:

  • understanding paraphrasing
  • identifying logical relationships
  • selecting grammatically correct endings
  • eliminating distractors
  • interpreting subtle meaning differences

Liz provides a full tutorial on this task type:
🔗 IELTS Liz – Matching Sentence Endings Guide

She highlights that this question type tests deep comprehension, not just scanning — exactly the direction IELTS is moving toward.

3. What IDP Experts Emphasise

IDP’s official Reading guidance reinforces the trend:

  • Matching tasks test idea relationships, not just keyword matching
  • Matching Sentence Endings requires paraphrasing recognition
  • TFNG is highly error-prone for candidates
  • Computer-delivered IELTS supports split-screen matching, making these tasks easier to navigate

IDP’s Reading skills page: 🔗 IDP – IELTS Reading Tips & Question Types

5. Why IELTS Is Making This Shift

Academic alignment

Matching tasks mirror the skills needed in university reading: connecting ideas, interpreting meaning, and understanding relationships.

Reduced ambiguity

TFNG questions often produce confusion and complaints, especially around “Not Given.”

Better fit for computer delivery

Matching tasks work beautifully with split-screen layouts.

More robust assessment of comprehension

Matching Sentence Endings tests deeper understanding than TFNG’s micro-comparison.

6. What Teachers Should Do

If you teach IELTS Reading (especially in Australia), update your materials:

Reduce TFNG-heavy worksheets

Still teach TFNG — but don’t overemphasise it.

Increase Matching Sentence Endings practice

Use passages with subtle distractors and similar vocabulary.

Teach paraphrasing as a core skill

Matching tasks rely heavily on meaning recognition.

Train students to use split-screen efficiently

This is now part of the test experience.
