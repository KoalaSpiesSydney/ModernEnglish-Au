Before you play: pre-lesson

A 15-minute lesson to teach normal (gradable) and extreme adjectives, the adverbs that go with them, and the championship and auction words. Project this section, or print it for the class.

Lesson steps (about 15 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (2 min). Show a photo of a marathon finish line. Ask: Are the runners tired? Very tired? Can we say “very exhausted”? Let students guess before you give the rule.
  2. The rule (4 min). Go through the table of adjective types and the adverbs that go with each.
  3. Pairs (3 min). Cover the right-hand column of the pairs table. Students say the extreme partner of each normal adjective.
  4. Quick check (3 min). Students choose the correct adverb in pairs, then check the answers.
  5. Championship and auction words (3 min). Teach the vocabulary and useful phrases, then start the game.

Quick check

Choose the correct adverb.

  1. The fans were (very / absolutely) excited before the match.
  2. The coach was (very / absolutely) furious with the referee.
  3. Her last jump was (really / very) amazing.
  4. The new stadium is (a bit / absolutely) huge.
  5. The sea was (extremely / absolutely) cold for the triathlon.
  6. Winning without training is (completely / slightly) impossible.
Show the answers

1 very (excited is normal) · 2 absolutely (furious is extreme) · 3 really (it works with both) · 4 absolutely (huge is extreme) · 5 extremely (cold is normal) · 6 completely (impossible is absolute)

About this game

Absolutely Champion is a free grammar auction for intermediate (B1) and upper-intermediate (B2) ESL classes on gradable and extreme adjectives. Teams become world-class sports teams and bid on sentences about the world’s biggest championships: the Olympic Games, the World Cup, Miss Universe and the World Happiness Report. Some sentences are forgeries, with an adverb and adjective that don’t go together, such as very boiling or absolutely cold.

At the reveal, the class votes on each sentence. A team that bought a forgery wins double points if it can correct it in the redemption round. For C1 and IELTS classes, play the harder version: Absolutely Genius.

Questions teachers ask

What’s an extreme adjective?

An extreme adjective already contains the idea of “very”: boiling means very hot, and exhausted means very tired. That’s why we say absolutely boiling, not very boiling.

Is “really” always correct?

Really works with both normal and extreme adjectives: really hot and really boiling are both fine. It’s a useful safe choice in speaking.

Which level should I choose?

Intermediate suits B1 classes and younger learners: the support stays on screen. Upper-intermediate suits B2 and FCE or IELTS preparation classes. For C1, use Absolutely Genius.

How to Play

Intermediate B1 · Upper-intermediate B2

Every sentence is for sale. Some are forgeries.

Teams become world-class sports teams and bid on sentences about the world’s biggest championships. A forgery pairs an adverb with the wrong kind of adjective, such as very boiling or absolutely cold.

The rule: normal (gradable) adjectives like cold, tired, funny go with very, extremely, a bit. Extreme adjectives like freezing, exhausted, hilarious already mean “very …”, so they go with absolutely. Really works with both.

One person is the auctioneer: the teacher or a student. The auctioneer runs this screen, reads each lot aloud, taps a team's paddle when they bid, and calls "Going once, going twice, sold!"

Read the catalogue first and agree as a team before you raise your paddle. Nobody finds out what they bought until the last lot is sold.

At the reveal, the class votes on each lot before the answer appears. A team that bought a forgery scores double points if it can correct the error in the redemption round.

Genuine lot = 1 point · corrected forgery = 2 points. The team with the most points tops the Rich List.
