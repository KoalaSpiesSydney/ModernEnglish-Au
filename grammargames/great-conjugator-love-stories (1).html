THE GREAT CONJUGATOR(Love Stories Edition)

About The Great Conjugator: Love Stories Edition

The Great Conjugator: Love Stories Edition is a free ESL game for B2–C1 classes that practises the perfect and continuous tenses we use to tell stories: the past continuous, the past perfect, the past perfect continuous, and the present perfect simple and continuous. Eight famous couples float across a starry night sky. Teams choose one and complete the love letter that tells how they met.

There are 23 love stories: films like When Harry Met Sally and Titanic, books and plays from Romeo and Juliet to Jane Eyre, real couples from Cleopatra and Mark Antony to Mary and Frederik of Denmark, and the Greek myth of Orpheus and Eurydice. Every round has a 90-second timer, a team that’s stuck can buy a hint for 5 points, and the final Fast Money round mixes tenses, negatives and questions.

Telling a love story: the tenses

TenseUse it forExample from the game
Past continuous
was / were + -ing		the background: what was in progress when something happenedRomeo first saw Juliet while he was wearing a mask at a party.
Past perfect
had + past participle		something that happened before another past eventJack was on the ship because he had won his ticket in a card game.
Past perfect continuous
had been + -ing		how long something had been going on before a past momentFiona had been waiting in the tower for years when Shrek rescued her.
Present perfect
have / has + past participle		a link between the past and nowMary and Frederik have been married since 2004.
Present perfect continuous
have / has been + -ing		an activity that started in the past and is still going onFans have been quoting the film’s funniest lines ever since.

Simple or continuous? Use the continuous for an activity and how long it lasted (had been writing to her every day for a year), and the simple for a result or a number (had written him more than 300 letters). State verbs such as know, love and be use the simple form: they had known each other for nine years, not had been knowing.

Using it in class (45 minutes)

  1. Warm-up (5 min). Ask: How did your parents or grandparents meet? What were they doing at the time? Had they ever met before? Board one story and label the tenses.
  2. The tenses (5 min). Go through the table above. Students put the five tenses on a timeline of one love story from the game.
  3. Play (25 min). Project the game and play in two to four teams. Before typing, a team must say which happened first, or what was already in progress.
  4. Fast Money (5 min). The hardest round: the same verb in three different tenses.
  5. Follow-up (5 min). Students write the story of how a famous couple, or a couple they know, met, using all five tenses at least once.

Short forms count in the game too: she’d been waiting, they’ve known, hadn’t / had not, wasn’t / was not. Where both the simple and the continuous are natural, both count.

The 23 love stories

Films

  • When Harry Met Sally – A 1989 American romantic comedy directed by Rob Reiner and written by Nora Ephron, about two friends who argue for twelve years about whether men and women can just be friends.
  • Titanic – A 1997 American film directed by James Cameron, about Jack and Rose, who fall in love on the ship’s first and last voyage.
  • Notting Hill – A 1999 British romantic comedy directed by Roger Michell and written by Richard Curtis, about a London bookshop owner who falls in love with a famous film star.
  • Sleepless in Seattle – A 1993 American romantic comedy directed by Nora Ephron, about a woman who falls for a man she has only heard on the radio.
  • The Notebook – A 2004 American film directed by Nick Cassavetes, based on the novel by Nicholas Sparks, about Noah and Allie, who meet at a summer carnival and never forget each other.
  • Muriel's Wedding – A 1994 Australian comedy written and directed by P. J. Hogan, about Muriel Heslop from Porpoise Spit, who dreams of the perfect wedding.
  • Shrek – A 2001 American animated film by DreamWorks, directed by Andrew Adamson and Vicky Jenson, about an ogre who rescues a princess from a dragon.
  • Past Lives – A 2023 Korean-American film written and directed by Celine Song, about two childhood sweethearts from Seoul who meet again in New York, 24 years later.
  • Marimar – A 1994 Mexican telenovela made by Televisa and starring Thalía, about a poor girl from the beach who marries the son of a rich landowner.
  • Amélie – A 2001 French film directed by Jean-Pierre Jeunet (original title: Le Fabuleux Destin d’Amélie Poulain), about a shy Paris waitress who secretly changes other people’s lives.
  • Your Name – A 2016 Japanese anime film by Makoto Shinkai (original title: Kimi no Na wa), about a country girl and a Tokyo boy who mysteriously swap bodies.

Books and plays

  • Romeo and Juliet – A tragedy by William Shakespeare, first performed in England in the 1590s, about two young lovers from families who hate each other.
  • Pride and Prejudice – An 1813 English novel by Jane Austen, about Elizabeth Bennet and the proud Mr Darcy.
  • Jane Eyre – An 1847 English novel by Charlotte Brontë, about a governess and the mysterious Mr Rochester.
  • Wuthering Heights – An 1847 English novel by Emily Brontë, about the wild, doomed love between Heathcliff and Catherine Earnshaw.
  • Love in the Time of Cholera – A 1985 Colombian novel by Gabriel García Márquez (original title: El amor en los tiempos del cólera), about a man who waits more than half a century for the woman he loves.

Real couples

  • Mary and Frederik of Denmark – A real couple from Australia and Denmark, who met in 2000: the Tasmanian who met a Danish prince in a Sydney pub during the Olympics, and is now Queen of Denmark.
  • Cleopatra and Mark Antony – A real couple from ancient Egypt and Rome, together from 41 to 30 BC: the Queen of Egypt and the Roman general whose love affair changed the history of the ancient world.
  • Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera – A real couple from Mexico, first married in 1929: the country’s most famous artists, who married twice and painted their way through a stormy love affair.
  • John Lennon and Yoko Ono – A real couple from Britain and Japan, who met in 1966: the Beatle and the Japanese artist who became the world’s most famous peace-protesting couple.
  • Victoria and Albert – A real couple from Britain, married in 1840: Queen Victoria and her German husband, Prince Albert, who had nine children.
  • The Taj Mahal – A real love story from India in the 1600s: the white marble tomb that the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan built for his wife, Mumtaz Mahal.

Myths

  • Orpheus and Eurydice – An ancient Greek myth, retold by the Roman poets Virgil and Ovid, about the musician who followed his wife into the underworld.
