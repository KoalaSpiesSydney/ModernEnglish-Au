About this game

To Be and Not to Being is a free state verbs game for elementary ESL classes (A1–A2). It works like a real auction: teams become wealthy families and bid on sentences they think are correct. Some sentences are forgeries, with a typical stative verb mistake such as I am knowing or This bag is belonging to me. Nobody finds out what they bought until the reveal, so every bid starts a discussion about the present simple vs present continuous.

The game gives learners a reason to read carefully, explain their choices and correct errors. Teams that bought a forgery can win double points by fixing it in the redemption round.

Questions teachers ask

What are state verbs?

State verbs (also called stative verbs) describe thoughts, feelings, senses and owning, not actions. Common examples are know, like, love, want, need, believe, understand, belong and have when it means own.

Why is “I am knowing” wrong?

Know is a state verb. It happens in your head, not as an action you can see, so it is not normally used in the present continuous. The correct sentence is “I know.”

Can state verbs ever take -ing?

Yes, when the verb has an action meaning. “I have a car” is a state (own), but “I am having lunch” is an action (eat). “I think English is easy” is an opinion, but “I am thinking about my holiday” is an action.

What level is this game for?

It is designed for elementary learners at CEFR A1–A2 who already know the present simple and are starting to use the present continuous.

How to Play

To be, and not to being · A1–A2

Every sentence is for sale. Some are forgeries.

Teams become wealthy families and bid on sentences they believe are genuine. A forgery hides a state verb mistake, such as I am knowing for I know.

One person is the auctioneer: the teacher or a student. The auctioneer runs this screen, reads each lot aloud, taps a family's paddle when they bid, and calls "Going once, going twice, sold!"

Read the catalogue first and agree as a team before you raise your paddle. Nobody finds out what they bought until the last lot is sold.

At the reveal, the class votes on each lot before the answer appears. A family that bought a forgery scores double points if it can correct the error in the redemption round.

Genuine lot = 1 point · corrected forgery = 2 points. The family with the most points tops the Rich List.
